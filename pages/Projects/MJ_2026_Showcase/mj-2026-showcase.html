MJ 2026 Showcase

Post-mortem on a one-minute, life-size AI dance performance of Michael Jackson — motion transfer, two-level LoRAs, and a tracked face pass.

  1. MJ Dancing Sequence
  2. Tech Stack
  3. Pipeline

The objective was a life-size video of Michael Jackson dancing, to be tested on a projection screen. It had to be more detailed and more realistic than previous iterations, and it had to run for a full minute — which turned out to be the hardest constraint of all. One minute of uninterrupted, fast, full-body motion is well beyond what most video models hold together, so the project became a study in keeping a character, a costume, and a face consistent across a single long take.

MJ Dancing Sequence

Preproduction

For the motion, a video and motion-capture session was run with two different dancers. The mocap material was never used: there wasn't time to clean it up, transfer it, and render it. Everything downstream was driven by the reference video instead — and that footage had problems worth designing out of the next shoot.

  • The dancers had a green screen behind them, but moved out of it.
  • The green screen itself is unnecessary for AI. A grey screen is the better choice now.
  • Both dancers had very different body proportions from MJ.
  • One dancer wore baggy pants and oversized clothing, the other hanging pants. Fitted clothes transfer far better through AI motion transfer.

Production

1. Video to video

A video reference drives a first frame of MJ. Three model families were tried for the motion transfer:

  • LTX IC-LoRA Control — as on previous projects, it didn't give good results for fast, complex body motion.
  • WAN ControlNet models — captured the motion very well, but are limited to 16 fps. An interpolation node doubling the frame rate solved that.
  • WAN SCAIL — although dated, the best option. It captured the motion perfectly and held it consistently.

The SCAIL Infinity node made the full one-minute generation possible, with minimal decay in the character or the costume across the take.

The unsolved problem: after about 20 seconds, SCAIL Infinity starts to show lighting changes and colour bleeding from the background. It was fixed in postproduction, but it remains the biggest issue in the pipeline.

2. LoRA training

Identity was split across two LoRAs, each doing the job the other couldn't:

  • Body LoRA. A SCAIL LoRA trained on the exact costume needed for the performance and added to the motion model. Good at HD resolution — but at full-body framing, the face lacked detail.
  • Head LoRA. A two-level LTX 2.3 LoRA trained on MJ's face, capturing his exact look, facial features, and hairstyle. Applied on a second pass.

3. Face swap workflow

A custom Griptape node tracked the face and exported it as a cropped video. That crop was upscaled and run through LTX Faceswap with the head LoRA to enhance likeness.

The faceswap's main weakness is that it loses consistency on fast motion — the default condition for a dance piece. The tracking step turned out to fix that almost by accident: because tracking the head also stabilises it, the faceswap received a steady, centred face instead of a fast-moving one, and the result was dramatically better.

The character LoRA also handled one of the hardest problems in the whole project: hair. MJ's hair looked natural, the strands falling over his face stayed consistent, and their motion was realistic.

Finally, the same Griptape tracking node composited the refined face back onto the original performance, using the positions stored during tracking.

Compared with Ozzy: the Ozzy sequences were image-plus-audio with prompted acting, so motion was never the constraint. Here the problem inverted — motion was given by the dancer, and the work was in holding identity through it.

Tech Stack

External Providers

ProviderRole
Wavespeed Access to most available inference models. Mostly used for static frame generation with NanoBanana, and for testing new models.
Runpod Hosting for dedicated ComfyUI workflows — so far the best and most economic option.
Griptape Orchestration. Building custom Griptape nodes for the face tracking and compositing proved incredibly useful.
Runcomfy Character LoRA training.

Software

  • DaVinci Resolve Studio
  • Adobe Photoshop
  • Shutter Encoder
  • Topaz Upscaler
  • Griptape Nodes

Models

  • NanoBanana — start image creation. Currently the best model for taking references, generating likeness, and prompt adherence. Used interactively.
  • WAN SCAIL — still one of the best models for motion transfer. Combined with a LoRA, the results were excellent, and the SCAIL Infinity node delivered long generations with minimal character drift.
  • KREA — fast to train, used for dataset creation as an intermediate step. A LoRA trained on real images produced a fully synthetic, curated dataset of face angles, expressions, and phonemes. Better than NanoBanana for large datasets, since it can be trained and even run locally.
  • LTX — the best model for realistic character creation. Character LoRAs trained on LTX have been excellent for likeness, even when trained on static material.

Pipeline

Two LoRAs are trained ahead of production. The dancer's performance drives a motion transfer on Runpod, which is upscaled, then handed to Griptape to track and isolate the face. The face crop is refined on Runpod with the head LoRA and composited back onto the performance using the stored tracking data.
