0c. Compatibility & Resolution Constraints

Why VAE, encoder, scheduler, and LoRA components cannot be freely swapped — and the resolution constraints baked into model weights during training.

The most common sources of errors and degraded output. The core rule: every component was co-trained or matched — mixing components across model families breaks assumptions baked into the weights. A dimension mismatch causes an immediate crash; a semantic mismatch causes silently degraded output.

VAE ↔ Diffusion Model

Why it mattersVAEs are NOT interchangeable between model families. Each model is trained end-to-end with a specific VAE, and the latent space encoding is baked into the diffusion model weights.
What breaksUsing the wrong VAE produces immediately visible problems: washed-out or oversaturated colours, loss of fine detail, blurry or distorted images. The 4-channel SDXL VAE and the 16-channel Flux VAE encode information completely differently — swapping them is like using the wrong codec to decode a video file. Even within SDXL, the original baked-in VAE has known FP16 precision issues — the fp16-fix VAE is the standard replacement. Flux.2 uses a different VAE than Flux.1.
Correct pairingsFlux.1: ae.safetensors  ·  Flux.2: flux2-vae.safetensors  ·  SDXL: sdxl-vae-fp16-fix.safetensors  ·  Qwen-Image: its own dedicated VAE  ·  Z-Image: Wan2.1 VAE (same team)

Text Encoder ↔ Diffusion Model

Why it mattersText encoders are architecture-specific. The diffusion model was trained to interpret conditioning from a specific encoder — feeding it vectors from a different encoder produces garbage or an immediate crash.
What breaksFlux.1 dev expects T5-XXL conditioning at 4096 dims. An SDXL CLIP encoder outputs 768 dims — the dimensions won't even match, causing an immediate crash. For Flux.1 you need BOTH CLIP-L and T5-XXL simultaneously via DualCLIPLoader. Flux.2 requires Mistral-3 — nothing else.
Correct pairingsFlux.1 [dev/schnell/kontext]: CLIP-L + T5-XXL (both required)  ·  Flux.2 [dev/klein]: Mistral-3 Small (24B)  ·  Z-Image: Qwen3-4B  ·  Qwen-Image-2512: Qwen2.5-VL-7B  ·  SDXL: CLIP-L + CLIP-G (both via DualCLIPLoader)

Scheduler ↔ Model

Why it mattersSchedulers must match the mathematical framework the model was trained with. DDPM-family schedulers (DPM++, Euler, DDIM, Karras) are incompatible with Flow Matching models (Flux).
What breaksDDPM models are trained to predict noise at discrete timesteps (0–999). Flow Matching models (Flux) use straight-line probability flows — the timesteps mean something completely different. Running DPM++ Karras on Flux produces degraded, incorrect results. SD3 and Flux share the same Flow Matching framework, so their schedulers overlap.
Correct pairingsSDXL: Euler a, DPM++ 2M Karras, DPM++ 3M SDE  ·  Flux.1 / Flux.2: Simple, SGM Uniform (not DPM++ / DDIM)  ·  SD3: same as Flux  ·  Qwen-Image: Euler + Simple (default), DPM++ 3S (faster)

LoRA ↔ Base Model

Why it mattersLoRAs encode weight offsets for a specific model architecture. A Flux.1 LoRA cannot be applied to SDXL, and vice versa. Even within Flux, a Flux.1 LoRA on Flux.2 is unreliable.
What breaksA LoRA file contains matrices shaped to match the exact weight dimensions of its target model. Applying a 12B Flux LoRA to a 3.5B SDXL model fails immediately — dimensions don't match. Multiple LoRAs stack fine within the same model but can conflict stylistically. Loading at strength >1.5 with any LoRA can produce artefacts.
Correct pairingsFlux.1 LoRA → Flux.1 only  ·  Flux.2 LoRA → Flux.2 only  ·  SDXL LoRA → SDXL only  ·  Strength: 0.5–1.0 safe, >1.5 risks artefacts  ·  No hard limit on simultaneous LoRAs but >3–4 often conflicts

Image Resolution Constraints

Resolution constraints come from how positional encoding is baked into model weights during training. Generating at the wrong size activates untrained positional combinations, producing artefacts, repeated subjects, or quality degradation.

Model Native / Optimal Multiple Behaviour Notes
SD 1.5 512 × 512 px Multiples of 64 Trained at 512px. Going above 768px causes repeating patterns. Use Hi-Res Fix or tiled upscaling to go higher. Never generate above 768×768 directly. The UNet's positional encoding breaks at larger sizes.
SDXL 1.0 1024 × 1024 px Multiples of 64 Can go to ~1536px on long edge without major issues. Above that, repeating artefacts appear. Non-square aspect ratios work well. Common sizes: 1024×1024, 1216×832, 896×1152, 1344×768. Always multiples of 64 or generation fails.
Flux.1 dev / schnell ~1024 × 1024 px Multiples of 16 Flexible resolution — can generate at various aspect ratios natively. Has been tested well beyond 1024px without artefacts. Much more flexible than SDXL. Quality degrades gracefully rather than producing artefacts.
Flux.2 dev ~1024 × 1024 px Multiples of 16 Same flexibility as Flux.1. The Mistral-3 text encoder supports 32K-token prompts — resolution flexibility matched by improved compositional quality. Pro Ultra mode via API reaches up to 4MP (~2048×2048).
Flux.1 Kontext ~1024 × 1024 px Multiples of 16 Editing model — resolution typically matched to the input image. Output generally matches the input to preserve coherence. When editing, keep output resolution matching input. Resizing during editing degrades identity preservation.
Z-Image Turbo ~1024 × 1024 px Multiples of 16 DiT architecture — flexible resolution. 16 GB VRAM comfortable at 1024px. S3-DiT architecture — resolution flexibility similar to Flux. No known hard ceiling.
Qwen-Image-2512 ~1328 × 1328 px Recommend multiples of 16 Officially supports: 1:1 (1328×1328), 16:9 (1664×928), 9:16 (928×1664), 4:3 (1472×1140), 3:2 (1584×1056). Use the official aspect ratio sizes where possible — trained on specific resolutions. Native 2K landscape (1664×928) is its strength.
The "multiples of 64" rule for SDXL/SD1.5 is hard — ComfyUI will round or error. The "multiples of 16" rule for DiT models (Flux, Qwen, Z-Image) is softer but still recommended. The 16-channel VAE in modern models provides better reconstruction at higher resolutions than the 4-channel SDXL VAE.
